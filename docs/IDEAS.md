# Haunted Hacks idea book: all 48 ideas, judged

Prepared **29 Oct 2026 (PT)**. 48 candidate ideas were generated from the research corpus ([research/SOURCE_RESEARCH_INDEX.md](research/SOURCE_RESEARCH_INDEX.md)) and scored 1-10 by six simulated judge personas. **The personas are models of likely judges, not real people's opinions**; their scores are a tool for finding tensions, not a forecast. The recommendation built from this is in [PLANNING.md](PLANNING.md).

Personas: **Terse** (Terse founder judging the Durable Actors track), **Relay** (Agent Relay founder judging the Relay track), **YC** (YC group partner, grand prize / interview), **Skeptic** (skeptical senior engineer: will it run in 5 hours?), **Supa** (Supabase DevRel, "scale to millions"), **Crowd** (demo-energy judge: does the room react?).

## 1. What the scores say

1. **No idea is strong with every judge.** The top average is 6.5. The two personas that matter for the *floor* prize (Terse, Crowd) reward audience contention games; the two that matter for *survival and the interview* (Skeptic, YC) reward simple, startup-shaped infrastructure. The winning plan has to graft feasibility and a buyer onto a crowd-pleasing contention core.
2. **Per-persona favourites diverge completely:** Terse → Midnight Soul Exchange (8.5); Relay → Ghost Ticket (8.5); YC → Witching Hour (8.0); Skeptic → Graveyard Shift / double-page (8.0); Supa → Possession Live (9.0); Crowd → Midnight Soul Exchange (9.0).
3. **Clusters, not ideas, are the real choice.** Six clusters cover 48 ideas (§3). The **contention/market** cluster dominates the top of the table; **rumor/provenance** is the most balanced across both hosts; **exorcist/firewall** and **crash-safe crews** have good startup stories but low crowd energy; **voice** and **coding factory** clusters fail the Skeptic badly.
4. **Supabase is a free 1-3 points when phones are the fan-out**, and a penalty when Supabase plays the villain in a race split screen.
5. **Ideas whose hero beat is a terminal kill score ≤4.5 with Crowd** (the Doom-arm lesson). Ideas whose hero beat is the room's phones score ≥7.5.

## 4. Each idea: pitch, hero beat, and why it scored as it did

Rationale lines condense the six persona notes; strongest praise first, main objection second.

### Contention / market

**1. Midnight Soul Exchange (6.50).** Thirty phones bid against four AI broker agents for cursed artifacts; brokers secretly form cartels over Relay DMs; each lot is an actor that takes bids one at a time. *Hero:* split screen "3 winners (naive) vs 1 winner, 412 bids, 0 conflicts"; kill every tab and the lot alarm still closes the auction; an "Antitrust Medium" replays the cartel DMs. *Praise:* Terse top pick ("the mailbox is the lock", alarm-closes-the-lot is the dormant aha, re-enacts the founding bug); Crowd 9 (most room energy); Supa 7 (anon RLS, httpSend, k6 load, honest breaking point); Relay 7 (heterogeneous colluding brokers, reveal mines Relay history). *Objections:* Skeptic 3 (~10 durable bids/s per lot over venue Wi-Fi plus too many beats); YC 4.5 (no buyer; Accord precedent); Relay: the hero beat is Terse's; Supa: naive baseline casts a Supabase row as villain.

**4. Trick-or-Trade (6.08).** Houses are actors with finite candy; five AI traders haggle by DM; the audience rushes one house. *Hero:* "0 oversold vs 14 oversold" live; a judge outbids an AI for the last bar. *Praise:* Skeptic 6 (scripted burst guarantees the number regardless of audience; Zod-constrained trades); Terse 7.5 (simple, legible). *Objections:* fifth candy-contention market; no dormant-alarm beat; no startup; Supabase-row villain.

**6. Full-Size Bar (5.83).** One full-size bar per house; four cross-vendor agents trade while 30 phones tap. *Hero:* 10-second porch-light rush, "duplicates 7 vs 0". *Praise:* Terse 8 (broad Crate-style primitive mapping: alarms, tag whispers, per-class `@Compute`); careful "don't claim Postgres can't lock rows". *Objections:* Skeptic 3 (wallet escrow, webhook bridge, 3 vendors, boss house).

**10. Trick-or-Treat Protocol, door to door (5.67).** Each attendee's agent trick-or-treats house actors overnight. *Praise:* textbook Terse beats, delightful costumes. *Objections:* one agent per attendee breaks the 5-agent cap; "close the laptop" beat is slow; YC 3.5.

**16. Knock Knock (5.58).** Houses judge costume photos with vision, gossip and blacklist by name. *Praise:* Crowd 8.5 (doubles as the costume contest); Supa 7 (most complete Supabase surface). *Objections:* Skeptic 3 (photo upload + vision per knock on venue Wi-Fi, moderation); Relay is flavour.

**25. Candy Cartel (5.08).** Each judge voices goals to a GPT-Live fixer; fixers haggle; one Exchange actor settles. *Praise:* Crowd 7.5 ("threaten him"). *Objections:* several simultaneous GPT-Live sessions, crosstalk, voice latency over negotiation; Trick-or-Trade does it cheaper.

**27. Trick-or-Treat Protocol, open porch (5.08).** Public houses any outside agent (a judge's Claude Code) can visit. *Praise:* Relay 7.5 (the only idea where outsiders' agents actually join). *Objections:* Skeptic 2 (needs outsiders and on-stage MCP config; self-hosted engine).

### Consumer / social

**2. The Curse (6.25).** Open a cursed link, a ghost latches on, haunts you on alarms with tabs closed; pass it on before the countdown ends; a live family tree with R0. *Praise:* Supa 8 (the viral loop *is* scale; lineage graph in Postgres; broadcast_changes; honest "Terse quotas are where it breaks"); Crowd 8.5 (whole room cursed by QR); Terse 7.5 (actor per victim, alarms while dormant, one-shot serialized claim). *Objections:* Relay 4.5 (coven bolted on; victims aren't Relay agents); YC 5 (no retention after Oct 31); Skeptic 4 (500-actor / 50-starts/s limits, phone onboarding on Wi-Fi).

**3. Familiars (6.08).** Ghost agents compete over Relay to "possess" humans for real-world tasks (photo of something orange); a vessel actor per human holds the lease. *Praise:* original twist on the host's slogan; Crowd 8.5 (judges physically moving); Terse 7.5 (two ghosts can't possess one person). *Objections:* Skeptic 3 (photo upload + vision over Wi-Fi; market looks dead without players); market novelty unchecked; dystopian optics.

**17. Possession Live (5.58).** Crowd votes the poltergeist's next scare; shard-actor fan-in tree; 2,000-voter load spike. *Praise:* Supa 9 (built for the prize: fan-in, httpSend fan-out, rollups, named breaking point). *Objections:* depends on undocumented actor-to-actor calls; Relay 3.5 (swappable); Skeptic 3.

**22. Ghost Pals (5.25).** Your persistent ghost visits friends' ghosts overnight, trades and gossips; morning card. *Praise:* cross-owner agents with durable offline delivery (Relay's least-explored claim). *Objections:* async by design, so the stage demo fakes time; dynamic workspace/agent registration per judge.

### Rumor / provenance

**5. Hearsay Hollow, watch a lie spread (5.92).** Four ghosts gossip over Relay DMs; audience whispers lies; a single-writer TownLedger actor records provenance; TRACE replays the chain; the Medium quarantines it. *Praise:* Relay 7 (DMs carry the lie, threads form the provenance chain, kill beat shows durable catch-up); Terse 7 (versioned ledger rejecting forged writes, midnight-bell alarm, `observe/state`); Crowd 8 (room-injected lie, TRACE to "phone #17"). *Objections:* AI Town drift; one of five rumor pitches; startup framing bolted on; Supa 4 (mirror cut first, no load).

**8. Rumor Mill (5.75).** Provenance plus a double-spent candy split screen. *Praise:* Terse 7.5 (pairs provenance with contention; correction spreads over the same Relay channel as the rumor). *Objections:* splits focus between two beats; crowded cluster.

**14. Ledger of the Dead (5.58).** Tighter rumor mill vs an unforgeable Book; one Claude ghost. *Praise:* Skeptic 6 (plain-list fallback, deterministic rejections). *Objections:* thinner duplicate of #5.

**15. Dead Men Tell Tales (5.58).** Maps nearly every Relay surface to a game mechanic (readers = "heard", group DMs = cabals, reactions = votes). *Praise:* Relay 7.5 (the Bitrig/Crate pattern applied to Relay). *Objections:* read-receipt semantics untested for SDK agents.

**18. Hearsay Hollow, ledger can't be gaslit (5.50).** Compare-and-set ledger, `assert_fact` typed action. *Praise:* smart narrow path. *Objections:* graph UI eats the hour; duplicate.

### Exorcist / firewall / safety

**11. Medium, cross-company A2A firewall (5.58).** Warden actor per protected agent; hash-chained log; Decisions classifier; quarantine with human release. *Hero:* "unauthorized actions N/10 → 0/10"; a tampered naive log goes unnoticed, the actor chain flags entry #7. *Praise:* Relay 8 (cross-org trust is the founders' favourite blog topic); YC 7.5 (sharpest why-now: Aug 26 HF incident, Terra eval; CISO buyer). *Objections:* CISO dashboard; actor is plumbing; Scene 1 fails if the unprotected agent refuses on stage; Crowd 4.

**19. Exorcist, live A2A firewall (5.42).** Most Relay-native safety idea (threads, reactions, steer, MCP actions, revocation). *Objections:* same-workspace only; three vendor stacks needless risk.

**23. Possession Protocol (5.17).** Sanctioned séance board with a sealed Medium. *Objections:* the research's consensus #1 idea, so other teams will build it.

**26. Exorcist, fleet supervisor (5.08).** OTP-style supervisor + loop breaker. *Praise:* Skeptic 7 (deterministic toggle). *Objections:* ops console, no room participation.

**29 / 30. Exorcist warden (5.00); Possessed exorcist for agent teams (4.83).** Policy-based quarantine; token rotation. *Objections:* interchangeable with the rest of the cluster; rotate-token untested; injection may not fire live.

### Crash-safe crews and B2B ops

**7. Probate (5.83).** Dead agents stay dead; their leases go through probate to heirs; alarm as dead-man switch; undeletable actors become queryable tombstones. *Praise:* Terse 7.5 (inverts the crowded resurrection demo; turns #228 into a feature); Skeptic 7. *Objections:* "just a supervisor"; Supa 3.

**9. Witching Hour (5.67).** Bring-your-own-agent incident room; intent ledger; judge approves from phone; kill -9 mid-rollback. *Praise:* YC 8 (real buyer, quotes Epstein's Multiplayer AI RFS, measured "duplicate remediations 2 vs 0"). *Objections:* Resolve AI ($1B) already sells a shared human + agent workspace; every agent is OpenAI so cross-vendor isn't demoed; Halloween is a skin; Crowd 4.5.

**12. Permitgeist (5.58).** Applicant agent negotiates with five city-department agents. *Praise:* YC 7 (live W27 "AI for City Hall" RFS; applicant-pays model validated by Noetic, CivCheck). *Objections:* every department simulated; five same-vendor LLMs look scripted; low adrenaline.

**13. Ghost Meter (5.58).** Race-free budget actor every agent asks before spending. *Praise:* Skeptic 7; YC 6.5 (October's loudest dev pain). *Objections:* gateways already offer budgets; Crowd 3.5 (Doom-arm trap).

**20. Graveyard Shift, can't double-page (5.42).** Lowest-risk Recipe B; real SMS lands once. *Praise:* Skeptic 8 (fewest moving parts). *Objections:* textbook infra demo; Supa 2.

**21. Phylactery (5.33).** Body = disposable process, soul = actor; SIGSTOP zombie rejected by lease check. *Praise:* Terse 7.5 (deepest correctness showcase). *Objections:* invisible infra; hand-rolled respawner.

**24. Ghost Ticket (5.08).** Customer and vendor agents debug across company lines; close a laptop lid, messages queue, catch-up on reopen. *Praise:* Relay 8.5 (literally their homepage use case). *Objections:* true cross-org unverified; reads as a support chatbot; Supa 2.

**28. Graveyard Shift, survive death (5.08).** Crash-safe cross-vendor crew with budget gate. *Objections:* least Halloween; overlaps four others.

**35. Medium, A2A debugging desk (4.58).** Targets Terse's cross-actor query gap. *Objections:* most moving parts; niche market.

**38. 3AM Pager (4.33).** Witching Hour plus voice. *Objections:* Skeptic 2.

## Limits

- Persona scores are simulated and correlated (same research corpus); treat a 0.5-point gap as noise.
- Market novelty was not checked beyond the corpus. "White space" = not found by our research.
- Throughput, quota and cap figures are documented defaults or self-reported, not measured by us.
